Performance ideas, roughly in priority order.
Benchmarks are in attic/benchmarks/;
build each with `cc -O2 FILE $(pkg-config --cflags --libs poppler-glib cairo) -lm`.
recording_surface.c needs an absolute path to the PDF, and writes PNGs to the current directory.

- Replay more kinds of PDF page from a Cairo recording surface.
  Pages with no strokes and at most one fill, paint, or mask are already replayed,
  which covers full-page scans.
  Their first render records them, and the preview and all later renders replay that.
  A tee also sends the page to an observer of a small image, to count its operations.
  Other pages then render directly, so that one extra pass slows their page turns.
  A transparency group counts as one operation, whatever's inside,
  so vector art that's entirely in one group is replayed, and drifts.
  Poppler re-decodes images on every render and caches nothing,
  so a scanned JPEG 2000 manual stalls for about 1 s,
  or 2 s for a fold-out, on the Pi after every zoom or pan without this.
  Memory grows by the decoded scan (about 11 MB a page, 22 MB for a spread).
  On the Pi (Cairo 1.16, Poppler 22.12), from attic/benchmarks/recording_surface.c:
    page 1:  record 790 ms;  direct 811 ms at 1x and 773 ms at 4x;
             replay 43 ms and 41 ms
    page 19 (fold-out):  record 1727 ms;  direct 1801 ms and 1722 ms;
             replay 84 ms and 41 ms
  Through the core on a Pi 5, a few PDFs and CBZs ran fast enough (2026-11-14);
  no timings were kept.
  What stops other pages: Cairo rounds a pure scaling transform to 24.8 fixed point (1/256)
  when it replays recorded paths.
  At 1.8x, a replayed rectangle lands exactly where a direct one would at 461/256,
  so edges at 90 and 540 land at about 90.04 and 540.24.
  Vector art drifts by up to about 1/512 of the page size in points
  (1.5 pixels at the far edge of an 800-point page, at any zoom),
  and abutting shapes get faint seams.
  Text was pixel-identical to a direct render in a test with three fonts.
  Rotated views are not affected either, since their matrices aren't pure scaling.
  Scales that are binary fractions (1.5, 2, 2.25) are exact; 1.35 and 1.8 aren't.
  A scan's image doesn't move, but Poppler draws it as a filled rectangle,
  so its right and bottom edges do, by under a pixel.
  In the laptop's Super Mario World manual, that changes the shade of the line of pixels
  at the page's edge, and the column at the seam between two pages.
  Replays also keep the image filter chosen when recording,
  so scans stay smooth at 8x, where a direct render would be blocky.
  attic/benchmarks/recording_scale.c reproduces the rounding with Cairo alone.
  A tiny shear (xy = 1e-12) in the replay transform avoids it,
  but it's a hack, and it may knock scans off pixman's fast scaled-image path;
  measure replay speed on a scan before using it.
  Options for pages with vector art: keep rendering them directly, use the shear,
  or report the rounding to Cairo upstream.
  Cairo's observer can't watch a recording surface.
  It writes a pixel to its target after each operation (_cairo_surface_sync),
  which on a recording becomes a visible square at the middle of the operation.
  Consider reporting that to Cairo upstream.
- Give each shown page its own preview, sized to the page, drawn with CAIRO_EXTEND_PAD.
  The preview is now one screen-sized image, clipped to the pages,
  and enlarging it blends each page's edge with whatever surrounds it in the image
  (white now; grey made the edges visibly grey).
  Separate previews fix the outer edges, the seam between two pages,
  and pages of different heights, and save memory.
  It also makes PDF match CBZ, which draws each page from its image with EXTEND_PAD.
  Simplest after the recording surface: with cheap replays,
  each preview can be a whole number of pixels with the page stretched under a pixel to fill it,
  and the view can always get a settled replay, even at fit-page zoom.
  Without recording, the fit-page view has to stay an exact pixel copy
  to avoid a second full render on every page turn,
  so previews have to start on whole pixels and partial edge pixels stay blended with white.
- Paint the grey background only outside the page, to stop painting pixels twice.
  The optimization branch's second commit does it, leaving out whole pixels that pages cover,
  and was about 10% faster a frame while panning at 4x on the laptop.
  It adds a fair amount of code, so it isn't worth it unless the Pi drops frames.
- Decode the next page in the background while the current one is shown,
  to hide page-turn cost when reading forward.
  Needs a worker thread and probably a second PopplerDocument,
  since Poppler isn't safe to share between threads.
- Render a margin around the view when it settles, and reuse it while panning nearby.
  While panning, the view is the fit-page preview enlarged, which is very blurry at 8x.
  A margin keeps panning sharp at high zoom without a full render per frame.
- Render on a worker thread that owns the backend, so a long render doesn't block retro_run,
  and with it RetroArch's menu and hotkeys.
  The backends keep their own state with no globals, which helps.
  Overlaps with decoding the next page in the background, above.
- Decide in the core, not with a per-backend flag, when a preview pays off.
  CBZ sets prefer_cached_preview to false,
  so every CBZ frame scales the full decoded image to the screen,
  with a bilinear filter during gestures, which aliases if the image is shrunk.
  A preview would avoid that, but it would blur enlarged pages,
  which CBZ can now draw sharply at full speed.
- Use nearest-neighbor filtering for the preview while zoom or pan buttons are held,
  if gestures can't hold 60 fps.
  About 3x faster than padded bilinear in attic/benchmarks/preview_scaling.c;
  the settled render replaces the blocky frame on release.
  The Pi already holds 60 fps while panning (7.6 ms average, 10.1 ms worst per frame),
  so this isn't needed yet.
- Hardware rendering through a libretro OpenGL context:
  upload each page's preview once as a texture and scale on the GPU.
  Removes the core's per-frame CPU work and RetroArch's frame copy
  (14% of samples in the laptop profile, in gl2_renderchain_copy_frame).
  The largest change; Pi 4 and 5 have OpenGL ES 3.1, Pi 3 and earlier ES 2.0.
- Scroll instead of redrawing while panning at a fixed zoom:
  shift the existing pixels and draw only the exposed strip.
  Doesn't help zooming, and adds complexity.
- Downscale high-resolution CBZ pages on load.
  Deferred until CBZ load times on the Pi show it matters;
  a few CBZs ran fast enough on a Pi 5.
  This would also fix pages over the 2^24-pixel cap, which now show a black placeholder;
  a letter-size page scanned at 600 dpi is about 34 megapixels.
  For JPEG, have libjpeg decode at 1/2, 1/4, or 1/8 size (scale_denom), which is cheap;
  for PNG, shrink rows as they're decoded.
  Keep reporting the original page size, so the layout doesn't change.
- Try RetroArch's glcore or vulkan video driver instead of gl,
  in case they upload frames more cheaply.  Unverified.

Ruled out:
- OpenJPEG threads (OPJ_NUM_THREADS, or opj_decompress -threads) barely help,
  and Poppler doesn't set a thread count.
- Decoding JPEG 2000 at reduced resolution is 4x faster,
  and half resolution suits a fit-page preview,
  but poppler-glib offers no way to request it.
- Detecting image-only pages to cache the scan directly isn't possible,
  because poppler-glib doesn't expose vector drawing.
  The recording surface makes it unnecessary.

Cleanups:
- Open documents through a stream instead of a path,
  so the core can use the frontend's virtual file system (libretro VFS).
  attic/android-notes.txt says Android needs it.
  Poppler-glib can open from bytes or a GInputStream,
  and libzip can read through a custom source.
  Cheaper to change now, while there are only two backends.
- List the supported extensions in one place.
  They're in the backend vtables, in valid_extensions in retro_get_system_info,
  and in docviewer_libretro.info.
- Share one extension helper:
  extension_matches in docviewer.c and extension_is in cbz_document.c do the same job.
- Check page dimensions in one layer.
  pdf_document.c rejects non-finite and non-positive sizes,
  and docviewer.c's load_page rejects sizes outside 0.01 to 1e8, with different messages.
- Give document_log_t a level.
  Backend messages all log at one level,
  and a page that fails to load is logged twice, by the backend and by the core.

Deferred features, once the basic versions work:
- CBZ pages in WebP and GIF.
- Natural sort for CBZ page names (page2 before page10), which comic readers expect.
  The README documents bytewise ordering now, so decide whether to change it.
- Fit-to-width zoom.
  A portrait page on a 16:9 screen fills less than half the width at fit-page zoom.
- Core options for the page mode and rotation,
  so RetroArch can save them per manual.
- Remember the page, zoom, and rotation for each manual,
  for example by exposing them as save RAM, which RetroArch saves to a .srm file.
